# AI in practice — nine cases for design leaders

Choose a case by the decision in front of you. Each entry describes a change in how a team worked and the outcome it measured. The [companion directory](ai-design-directory.md) introduces the people behind these approaches and their original work.

## Leading in the age of AI

Where should designers, engineers, and specialists work together when AI changes who can do the work?

- **[Adyen — reviewing real components while a UI is being built](https://www.figma.com/customers/how-adyen-is-rebuilding-design/)**  
  Designers and engineers reviewed generated interfaces together using Adyen’s production components and tokens. Selected UI builds went from two or three engineering days to about two hours of joint work. In their [workflow presentation](https://www.figma.com/webinars/workflow-lab-adyen-mcp/), Hong Zou and Hanbin Chang explain where early collaboration changed a design decision.

- **[trivago — connecting UX writing and localization](https://tech.trivago.com/post/2026-10-02-from-tools-to-teammates-how-ai-empowered-our-ux-team-to-level-up)**  
  Ankia Wolf, Sami Hammami, Dan Ardeleanu, and colleagues linked copy drafting, localization keys, and translation requests in one workflow. The UX team reports 75% shorter writing turnaround and 41% more output. It says quality scores stayed high and describes checks for terminology, placeholders, and character limits. The scoring rubric is unpublished.

## Defining and measuring impact

What would convince you that a faster workflow produced better work? These teams measured elapsed time alongside a separate quality or performance outcome.

- **[Procter & Gamble — measuring the quality of early product ideas](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33641/w33641.pdf)**  
  In a randomized workshop with 776 professionals, individuals using AI spent 16.4% less time on real innovation briefs than unaided individuals. Judges who did not know how each proposal was made scored AI-assisted proposals 0.37 standard deviations higher on average. The experiment measured ideas before commercialization.

- **[Intuit TurboTax — testing campaign creative against a control](https://business.adobe.com/blog/real-time-insights-and-on-demand-creative-for-turbotax)**  
  After a first pilot missed its performance target, the team used Adobe GenStudio for ad assembly, copy, and refreshes. Adobe reports turnaround went from up to two weeks to as little as four hours. In the second pilot, treatment ads achieved 38% higher click-through than controls.

## Craft and quality

Which parts of the work deserve an expert review, and which can the team protect through its production process?

- **[BCG — judging an idea separately from its presentation](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838)**  
  On a footwear brief, consultants using GPT-4 without additional training reached the final question 27.6% faster than controls. Human graders scored their overall work 29.9% higher. When footwear specialists judged only the ideas for creativity, the advantage was 11.7%. The choice of reviewer and criterion changed the result.

- **[Unilever — preserving packaging fidelity in generated campaign images](https://www.unilever.com/news/press-and-media/press-releases/2025/unilever-reinvents-product-shoots-with-digital-twins-and-ai/)**  
  Creative teams reused approved 3D models of products, including their labels and local variants, while changing the surrounding scene. For TRESemmé Thailand, Unilever reports twice the production speed, 87% lower content costs, and a 5% increase in purchase intent. That last figure measures stated preference.

## Customer value and trust

What can a customer do differently, and what evidence shows the change mattered?

- **[Mitsubishi Electric — letting clients change a prototype during the meeting](https://www.figma.com/customers/mitsubishi-electric-corporation/)**  
  Using Serendie design-system components with Figma Make, one app team cut prototype build time from two weeks to one day. It held 12 client and internal demos, up from three, while validation fell from ten weeks to six. The team measured opportunities for feedback and time to validation.

- **[Microsoft Copilot — asking customers to judge responses to their own prompts](https://outset.ai/resources/stories/how-microsoft-increased-copilot-retention-by-five-percent-with-outset)**  
  Christopher Monnier’s team combined human interviews with AI-moderated sessions reaching thousands in days. Monnier, Chuck Kwong, and Wendy Wang describe how their [research method](https://microsoft.design/articles/putting-the-user-back-into-ai-evaluation/) connects users’ explanations to specific responses. Microsoft internally estimated that subsequent product changes raised retention by roughly 5%. The public case gives no comparison group or retention window.

- **[Duolingo — widening access to listening practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/scaling-duoradio/)**  
  Learning designers grounded scripts in existing curricula and built checks for language level and coherence. DuoRadio grew from 300 to over 15,000 episodes in fewer than two quarters. Daily listening sessions rose from 500,000 to five million. That shows increased use as access expanded. Learning gains would require a separate measure.
