# Design leadership × AI

A curated guide to people, companies, and practices worth studying at the intersection of design and AI.

Built for design leaders deciding where to invest, how teams should work, how to preserve craft, and how to measure value. Each entry explains **why it belongs**, links directly to the relevant work, and identifies something a leader can learn or apply.

## Start here

- **[People, companies, and practices — 44 entries](guides/ai-design-directory.md)**  
  Discover designers, researchers, teams, and perspectives organized around leadership challenges. Use this guide to find work to follow and approaches to examine.

- **[Companies in practice — 9 cases](guides/ai-design-company-cases.md)**  
  Explore changes to design workflows, creative production, user research, and learning experiences. Each case explains the reason for inclusion, reported results, and relevant limits.

## Explore by leadership challenge

Both guides use four shared pillars.

- **[Leading in the age of AI](guides/ai-design-directory.md#leading-in-the-age-of-ai)**  
  Choose worthwhile problems, reshape responsibilities, and decide how designers, engineers, and specialists should collaborate.

- **[Defining and measuring impact](guides/ai-design-directory.md#defining-and-measuring-impact)**  
  Establish what improved, for whom, and against which baseline. Assess speed alongside quality, customer response, and the work required to finish.

- **[Craft and quality](guides/ai-design-directory.md#craft-and-quality)**  
  Protect originality, consistency, and design intent. Examine how design systems, expert critique, and creative controls shape generated work.

- **[Customer value and trust](guides/ai-design-directory.md#customer-value-and-trust)**  
  Design experiences that help people act, understand, verify, and recover. Connect technical capability to a useful customer outcome.

## How to use the guide

1. Start with a decision your team faces and choose the relevant pillar.
2. Read the rationale, then follow the linked title to inspect the original work.
3. Select a practice to explore. Define the owner, quality standard, and outcome you would measure before scaling it.

## Selection and evidence

Entries earn their place through a clear connection to design, a useful leadership question, and work that readers can inspect. A company’s inclusion applies to the practice described. The rationale is an editorial judgment grounded in the linked material.

Read the evidence according to its form.

- **Measured outcomes** come from structured evaluations. Results apply to the participants, tasks, and conditions studied.
- **Company-reported cases** describe operating changes and reported results. Some are published by technology vendors.
- **Prototype and research references** contribute methods or interaction patterns. Their value may precede demonstrated business outcomes.
- **Adjacent strategy** helps leaders frame investment or organizational decisions. These entries are included for their relevance to design leadership.
