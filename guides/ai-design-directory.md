# A design leader’s field guide to AI

[Guide overview](../README.md)

Where should design invest in AI, and what would make that investment worthwhile? This directory selects people and companies for specific contributions to design practice. Each rationale explains the leadership challenge, the approach worth learning, and the evidence behind the choice. Adjacent strategy references are identified in the text. The [nine company case studies](ai-design-company-cases.md) focus on changes in how design work gets done.

- **[Leading in the age of AI](#leading-in-the-age-of-ai)** — Choose investments, shape roles, and agree on what teams share.
- **[Defining and measuring impact](#defining-and-measuring-impact)** — Decide what success means and what evidence would justify scaling.
- **[Craft and quality](#craft-and-quality)** — Preserve design intent, originality, and consistency as output grows.
- **[Customer value and trust](#customer-value-and-trust)** — Build experiences people can use, verify, and control.

## Leading in the age of AI

How should we choose what to build, divide the work, and spend the time AI gives back? The goal is a team that reaches better decisions and knows who owns them.

### Which bets deserve the team’s time?

**[Rachel Kobetz — deciding what design leadership owns](https://kobewan.substack.com/p/dx-perspective-whats-worth-making)**

- **Why it belongs.** A strategic beacon for deciding where design leadership adds value when AI expands what a team can build. Kobetz connects product bets, coherent experiences across tools, and agents acting for customers. Use her questions to set AI investment priorities and decide which judgments design should own.

**[Jasmine and Tanner Christensen — explaining design’s contribution to the business](https://newlayerpodcast.com/episodes/2026/11/where-does-design-go-from-here/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Useful for explaining design’s contribution as AI expands who can generate concepts. Their discussion of Mills Baker’s [Designer Duds](https://medium.com/@millsbaker/designer-duds-f59c964513ef) ties design value to problem selection and business judgment. Use it to reset team goals around stronger product decisions and customer outcomes as producing variants becomes easier.

**[Filip Kaliszan, Tamar Yehoshua, and Maddi Holman — funding a second product bet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n073LFOjcwM)**

- **Why it belongs.** An adjacent strategy reference for design leaders whose AI prototypes create more possible bets than the organization can fund. These leaders’ accounts of customer demand opening new markets help connect discovery to investment. Apply their questions to AI product concepts. The source documents product expansion and provides no measured AI design outcomes.

### Who should build, critique, and approve the work?

**[Anthropic — bringing designers into production in their first week](https://designerfund.substack.com/p/ai-design-anthropic)**

- **Why it belongs.** A reference for reshaping design roles and critique as designers ship code. Joel Lewenstein, Meaghan Choi, Nate Parrott, Kyle Turman, and Matt Gallivan describe reviews up to four times a week. The [team’s account](https://stateofaidesign.com/cases/anthropic) gives leaders a concrete operating model to inspect. Its 3–5× design-speed estimate is illustrative.

**[Jenny Wen — carrying a product vision into implementation](https://jennywen.ca/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Shows how a design leader can set an AI product’s direction while staying close to implementation. Wen’s Cowork work linked product vision, internal prototypes, and code contributions around launch. Her [interview on design leadership](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-design-process-is-dead) helps leaders rethink the balance between directing teams, making, and reviewing working software.

**[Adyen — reviewing real components while a UI is being built](https://www.figma.com/customers/how-adyen-is-rebuilding-design/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Included for changing how design and engineering make decisions together. Connecting Figma frames to production components and tokens let both disciplines review working interfaces during the build. Adyen reports selected builds fell from two or three days to about two hours. Study it when redesigning handoffs while preserving design-system fidelity.

**[trivago — connecting UX writing and localization](https://tech.trivago.com/post/2026-10-02-from-tools-to-teammates-how-ai-empowered-our-ux-team-to-level-up)**

- **Why it belongs.** Shows how design operations can reduce handoffs across UX writing and localization. Its connected drafting, translation-key, and translation-request workflow reports 75% shorter writing turnaround and 41% more output. Useful for content-design leaders scaling multilingual experiences. The team says quality stayed high, with no published scoring rubric.

### What should embedded teams share?

**[Stuart Frisby — giving teams common rules across different tools](https://www.designleadershipmemos.com/p/x-is-dead-is-dead)**

- **Why it belongs.** A leadership reference for preserving a coherent product as AI lets teams choose different tools and workflows. Frisby identifies shared product context, components, and decision rules as the work leaders must organize. Use it to decide what belongs in a common design foundation and where teams can experiment independently.

**[Ramiro Galan and Adam Cutler — co-designing IBM’s AI standard](https://pixelkings.com/work/ibm-c4ai/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A beacon for turning AI trust requirements into reusable design-system components. The [Carbon for AI leads](https://juan.design/work/carbon-for-ai/) worked with 12 early-adopter teams, including on an [explanation component](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/carbon-for-ai) beside AI-generated fields. Useful for design-system leaders who need shared disclosure and explanation patterns that embedded product teams can adopt.

**[Julien Clément and Antoine Deshoux — sharing specialist agents at Carrefour](https://www.figma.com/customers/how-carrefour-leveraged-ai-to-drive-design-delivery/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Shows how a central design team can spread useful AI practices across embedded teams. Carrefour lets designers propose, test, and publish specialist agents, with more than 50 approved. Useful for leaders building an internal enablement program. Reported 80% weekly Figma Make use measures adoption. Delivery impact needs separate evidence.

## Defining and measuring impact

What improved, for whom, and compared with what? The goal is to decide whether an AI workflow merits further investment using evidence that covers the work from brief to delivery.

### What would count as a better result?

**[Procter & Gamble — testing whether faster ideas are also better ideas](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33641/w33641.pdf)**

- **Why it belongs.** An evidence reference for design leaders evaluating AI-assisted concept development. In a randomized workshop with 776 professionals, AI-assisted individuals used 16.4% less time and earned blind expert scores 0.37 standard deviations higher than unaided individuals. Borrow the comparison group and independent quality assessment when setting pilot criteria. Results cover proposals before commercialization.

**[Intuit TurboTax — testing whether customers respond to faster creative work](https://business.adobe.com/blog/real-time-insights-and-on-demand-creative-for-turbotax)**

- **Why it belongs.** Included because it connects AI-assisted creative production to customer response through a controlled campaign test. After a first pilot missed its target, turnaround fell from up to two weeks to as little as four hours, with 38% higher click-through than control ads. Useful for design leaders pairing production metrics with audience-response measures.

**[Figma and MeasuringU — establishing a baseline for prototyping time](https://www.figma.com/blog/measuring-time-savings-from-figma-make/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Useful for leaders establishing a baseline before changing design workflows. A randomized study assigned 100 designers and product managers to interface tasks. Among completers, access to Figma Make reduced time by about 20%. Borrow its comparison structure and add quality criteria, which this study left open.

### What does the headline metric leave out?

**[Stephanie Nyairo — measuring the work between a draft and a deliverable](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/making-generative-ai-workable-at-work-accountability-localization-and-representation-in-kenyan-and-nigerian-small-businesses/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A beacon for measuring the finishing work behind usable AI output. The Nairobi-based [product designer](https://www.stephanienyairo.com/) and colleagues interviewed 27 workers across 21 Kenyan and Nigerian businesses about checking claims, recovering sources, and repairing local language. Use the findings to include verification and localization in design productivity estimates and staffing plans.

**[Nitin Nohria and Hélène Hannecart — finding the bottleneck after delegation](https://hbr.org/2026/11/the-hidden-costs-of-delegating-to-ai)**

- **Why it belongs.** An adjacent operations reference for leaders scaling AI-assisted design. Their account of four accounting firms using AI for tax preparation shows how faster tasks can increase review demands and change junior learning. Apply the questions to design critique capacity, mentoring, and time to delivery. HBR provides a public summary.

**[Ethan Mollick — checking whether an evaluation rewards the right thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi)**

- **Why it belongs.** An evaluation reference for design leaders choosing what to optimize. Mollick’s agents generated virtual worlds and a scoring system, while he still had to judge whether the experience was enjoyable. Use the example to question whether an AI quality score captures the customer experience your design team intends to create.

## Craft and quality

How do we protect consistency, taste, and originality as output grows? The goal is to give makers precise control and reviewers a repeatable way to recognize work worth shipping.

### Will the output hold up in production?

**[Jordan Singer — generating with the components a team actually owns](https://ibuildmyideas.substack.com/p/a-tricycle-for-our-minds)**

- **Why it belongs.** A pioneer in AI-assisted interface design whose work links generation to reusable components. Singer demonstrated a GPT-3 Figma plugin in 2020, then his Diagram team built [Genius](https://www.figma.com/blog/the-future-of-design-systems-is-automated/) to suggest existing components. Useful for leaders evaluating whether AI tools can carry their design system into generated work and reduce review effort.

**[David Sward, Marianne Ashton-Booth, and Nicolas Chatelain — scaling design review](https://uxdesign.cc/what-ai-has-changed-in-design-systems-and-what-is-still-up-to-us-d4e8e8f4ceb2)**

- **Why it belongs.** Useful for design-system leaders facing a surge in AI-generated variants and decisions. These interviews examine who approves new patterns and how shared standards evolve. The [Siemens case](https://radar.zurb.com/article/siemens-design-chief-my-biggest-problem-is-trying-to-slow-people-down-with-ai) makes review capacity a leadership issue. Use it to clarify approval ownership and which generated changes require expert attention.

**[Graphical — packaging a design system for a coding agent](https://www.graphicalui.com/)**

- **Why it belongs.** An emerging tool to inspect when making a design system usable by coding agents. Graphical bundles tokens, React components, and a `GUI.md` guide covering behavior alongside visual styling. Useful for design leaders specifying what agents need to produce consistent interfaces. The evidence here is a product description.

**[Unilever — protecting packaging accuracy in generated campaign images](https://www.unilever.com/news/press-and-media/press-releases/2025/unilever-reinvents-product-shoots-with-digital-twins-and-ai/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A production example for protecting brand craft while using AI for variation. Approved 3D product models preserve packaging as scenes change. For TRESemmé Thailand, Unilever reports 2× production speed, 87% lower content costs, and a 5% increase in purchase intent. Useful for creative leaders deciding which assets stay controlled and which can be generated.

### How do we judge the idea as well as the execution?

**[Jason Wu and Amanda Swearngin — teaching a UI generator through critique](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/designer-feedback)**

- **Why it belongs.** A research beacon for making design judgment part of model improvement. Their team collected roughly 1,500 annotations from 21 designers, and models trained with detailed feedback outperformed a ranking baseline in their evaluation. Useful for design leaders building critique rubrics or evaluation datasets that explain which UI elements need to change.

**[Boston Consulting Group — separating the quality of a pitch from the originality of its idea](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838)**

- **Why it belongs.** An evidence reference for separating concept originality from presentation quality in AI-assisted design. On a footwear brief, GPT-4 users without additional training reached the final task 27.6% faster, with 29.9% higher overall scores and 11.7% higher specialist creativity scores. Useful for leaders choosing reviewers and defining multiple dimensions of quality.

**[Dylan Field and David Friedberg — making a point of view visible in the work](https://www.figma.com/blog/in-conversation-dylan-field-and-david-friedberg/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Useful for leaders preserving a product’s point of view when AI supplies the first draft. Field describes changing an AI-generated starting point to discover which choices matter to him. Bring the discussion into critique to ask which decisions express the product’s purpose, audience, and distinctive character.

**[Patrick Neeman — bringing a model’s worst answer into critique](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-is-the-new-old-design-material-0e7f83540c9a)**

- **Why it belongs.** A practical beacon for reviewing AI behavior as part of product craft. Neeman proposes examining repeated outputs and bringing poor examples into critique. Use the approach to assess variability, recovery, and the customer’s next step before shipping. It helps a design team review how the experience behaves beyond a polished example.

### Where can a maker exercise finer control?

**[Rebecca Fiebrink — teaching an instrument by playing it](https://nime.org/proc/nime2009_fiebrink/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A foundational reference for interaction design that lets people train AI through use. With Dan Trueman and Perry Cook, [Fiebrink](https://www.arts.ac.uk/research/ual-staff-researchers/rebecca-fiebrink) built Wekinator so musicians could demonstrate gestures, hear results, and revise examples immediately. Useful for leaders exploring direct manipulation, learnability, and user control in AI creative tools.

**[Bjørn Karmann — giving model settings physical controls](https://bjoernkarmann.dk/project/objectifier)**

- **Why it belongs.** A design beacon for giving people tangible control over AI behavior. Objectifier lets users teach a device through gestures, while [Paragraphica](https://bjoernkarmann.dk/project/paragraphica) exposes settings through physical dials. Study these prototypes when your team needs alternatives to prompting and settings panels. They demonstrate interaction possibilities at the prototype stage.

**[Sougwen Chung — keeping authorship visible in human–machine collaboration](https://sougwen.com/work/memory-drawing-operations)**

- **Why it belongs.** A creative reference for preserving authorship in human–AI collaboration. Chung trained MEMORY on 20 years of their own drawings and fed later joint work into the archive. The [V&A interview](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/digital/the-algorithmic-gesture-sougwen-chungs-memory) makes the artist’s ongoing decisions visible. Useful for creative leaders defining the roles of personal material, curation, and human judgment.

**[Shan Carter and Michael Nielsen — inventing a control through examples](https://distill.pub/2017/aia/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A foundational interaction-design reference for helping people express intent without precise prompts. Their essay lets readers choose font examples and manipulate a slider learned from those choices. Useful for design leaders exploring AI controls based on examples and direct feedback, especially when users recognize what they want but cannot describe it.

## Customer value and trust

What does AI help a customer do, and what should stay under their control? The goal is an experience customers can use, question, and correct when the stakes matter.

### What becomes possible for the customer?

**[Mitsubishi Electric — letting clients revise a prototype in the meeting](https://www.figma.com/customers/mitsubishi-electric-corporation/)**

- **Why it belongs.** Included for using AI prototyping to increase customer participation in design decisions. On one app project, prototype development fell from two weeks to one day, demos increased from three to 12, and validation fell from ten to six weeks. Useful for leaders shortening the path from assumptions to feedback. These are process measures.

**[Duolingo — expanding access to listening practice](https://blog.duolingo.com/scaling-duoradio/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A learning-design example of turning AI output into a structured customer experience. Designers grounded scripts in curricula and checked level and coherence before scaling DuoRadio from 300 to 15,000+ episodes. Daily sessions grew from 500,000 to five million. Useful for leaders balancing expert content standards with broader access. Learning gains need separate evaluation.

**[Qian Yang — fitting AI into a decision people already make](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/unremarkable-ai-fitting-intelligent-decision-support-into-critical-clinical-decision-making-processes)**

- **Why it belongs.** A research beacon for designing AI around existing human decisions and collaboration. Yang’s team placed machine prognoses in slides clinicians already discussed, preserving space for group deliberation. Her [Cornell research group](https://designai.cis.cornell.edu/) helps design leaders frame workflow fit and reliable use as core design problems when a model’s capabilities remain uncertain.

**[Cecily Morrison — helping a blind child initiate a conversation](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/peoplelens-using-ai-to-support-social-interaction-between-children-who-are-blind-and-their-peers/)**

- **Why it belongs.** An inclusive-design beacon for defining AI value through a person’s agency. PeopleLens uses spoken names to help a wearer choose whom to approach. The [research with a blind child](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/social-sensemaking-with-ai-designing-an-open-ended-ai-experience-with-a-blind-child/) examines an open-ended social experience. Useful for leaders setting goals around participation and independence, then evaluating the whole interaction alongside model performance.

**[Archana Prasad — reaching people in their own language and channel](https://www.help.gooey.ai/farmerchat)**

- **Why it belongs.** A beacon for designing AI access around local language, voice, and familiar channels. Prasad cofounded Gooey.AI, which worked with Digital Green on Farmer.CHAT. Her [design research](https://www.rca.ac.uk/research-innovation/research-degrees/research-students/archana-prasad/) explores community influence over knowledge in AI. Useful for leaders planning inclusive research and service delivery across markets with different languages and access needs.

### Can people check an answer or challenge a decision?

**[Microsoft Copilot — learning what makes an answer worth coming back for](https://outset.ai/resources/stories/how-microsoft-increased-copilot-retention-by-five-percent-with-outset)**

- **Why it belongs.** Included for connecting AI product evaluation to customers’ own prompts and judgments. Researchers asked people to [compare answers to their own prompts](https://microsoft.design/articles/putting-the-user-back-into-ai-evaluation/) and explain usefulness, then used findings to guide product changes. Useful for leaders building evaluation around user needs. Microsoft’s roughly 5% retention estimate lacks a published comparison method and time window.

**[Jason Spielman — keeping a claim and its source visible together](https://jasonspielman.com/notebooklm)**

- **Why it belongs.** A product-design beacon for making verification part of an AI experience. NotebookLM’s early design lead shows how sources, chat, and output were organized so readers could check a citation while keeping the claim visible. Useful for design leaders reviewing trust, information hierarchy, and the effort required to validate generated answers.

**[Min Kyung Lee — letting affected people help write an algorithm’s rules](https://minlee.ischool.utexas.edu/materials/Publication/2019-CSCW-AI_Participation.pdf)**

- **Why it belongs.** A beacon for involving affected people in the design of AI decision rules. Donors, recipients, staff, and volunteers helped shape WeBuildAI’s allocations. A five-month retrospective simulation shifted donations toward higher-poverty areas and reduced median travel from 6.15 to 5.5 miles. Lee’s [Stanford talk](https://hci.cs.stanford.edu/courses/cs547/speaker.php?date=2019-01-18) helps leaders plan participatory design and examine whose outcomes improve.

**[Michelle S. Lam — giving people the means to audit a model](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=470)**

- **Why it belongs.** A research beacon for bringing users’ judgments into AI evaluation. In [Lam’s](https://michelle123lam.github.io/) IndieLabel study, 17 nontechnical participants uncovered toxicity-model errors involving veiled hate and reclaimed language. Useful for design leaders creating participatory tests that expose harms a predefined benchmark may miss and make disagreement visible before deciding how to respond.

**[Saleema Amershi and collaborators — designing for the moment AI gets it wrong](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A foundational reference for making trust and recovery explicit design requirements. The guidelines cover first use, mistakes, and adaptation over time. Useful for design leaders turning responsible-AI intentions into acceptance criteria, critique questions, and test scenarios for how customers understand, correct, or recover from system behavior.

### What should a customer delegate to an agent?

**[Emily Campbell — defining what an agent must ask before it acts](https://www.shapeof.ai/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A practical beacon for translating agent capabilities into interaction decisions. The Shape of AI catalogs consent, memory, and confirmation patterns, while Campbell’s [framework for AI experiences](https://shapeofai.substack.com/p/designing-beneath-the-surface-the) distinguishes interface choices from model context and permitted actions. Useful for leaders reviewing what an agent can do, when it must ask, and how customers retain control.

**[Maggie Appleton — giving collaborators a say in what an agent changes](https://githubnext.com/projects/chopin/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A design research beacon for making collaboration with agents visible. Appleton’s [Language Model Sketchbook](https://maggieappleton.com/lm-sketchbook) explores an editor that challenges claims. Chopin lets people discuss text and ask an agent to revise it together. Useful for leaders examining shared intent, authorship, and who can approve changes in collaborative AI products.

**[Raluca Budiu — studying the handoff between a person and an agent](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-vs-experience-design/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A UX research reference for adapting evaluation to AI agents. Budiu distinguishes people using an interface, agents acting on their behalf, and both working together. Useful for design leaders deciding who or what a journey serves, then testing handoffs, interruption, correction, and the customer’s ability to understand the result.

**[Olivia Moore and Justine Moore — designing for a buyer represented by software](https://a16z.com/welcome-to-the-b2a-economy/)**

- **Why it belongs.** An adjacent commerce reference for design leaders whose customers may delegate shopping to agents. Their analysis, alongside [Stripe and OpenAI’s commerce protocol](https://stripe.com/blog/developing-an-open-standard-for-agentic-commerce), clarifies how discovery and checkout may involve a buyer, merchant, and agent. Use it to map consent, identity, information needs, and recovery across the journey before defining the interface.

**[DDX — asking who will read, choose, or transact on a customer’s behalf](https://www.ddxconference.com/research/designing-for-the-machine-57-leaders)**

- **Why it belongs.** A directional signal for leaders designing products that agents may read or use. Eight of 57 leaders independently raised agents in an open question about innovation. The discussion helps frame discovery questions about delegation and product legibility. Treat it as input for design research, with a small, qualitative evidence base.

**[Tony Fadell — deciding whether an AI device earns a place in someone’s life](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/tony-fadell-on-why-the-first-wave-of-ai-gadgets-failed-and-what-comes-next/)**

- **Why it belongs.** A product-judgment reference for design leaders considering AI devices or ambient assistants. Fadell’s reflections on Rabbit, Humane, and Limitless center on everyday usefulness and the trust required to grant sensitive access. Use those questions in concept reviews to test whether a new form factor earns adoption and whether its permissions fit the value offered.

*Sources checked November 17, 2026.*
